Record Your Family's Stories in Armstrong

Most of us have a parent or a grandparent who tells the same stories every time we see them. We all mean to record them one day. Almost nobody does.

How it works

You bring them in and the two of you sit down and talk. You ask the questions, not me. Stories come out different for family than they do for a stranger, and that's most of why this works. I'm in the background on the cameras and the sound, so neither of you has to think about any of that. You leave with the recording the same day.

Questions

What do we talk about?

Whatever you'd ask if the camera wasn't there. How they met. What their first job paid. What this town looked like when they got here. The story you've heard a hundred times and have never once recorded. If you want a hand putting a list together before you come in, ask me and we'll do that.

Will they be comfortable?

Most people are, faster than they expect, because they're talking to you and not to a camera. The first few minutes are the two of you settling in, and none of that has to be used. That part is normal.

What if they say no?

Some people do at first. It usually helps to frame it as something for the grandkids rather than something about them.

How soon should we do this?

Sooner than feels necessary. That's the honest answer.
